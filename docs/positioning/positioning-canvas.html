The positioning statement

For lean teams with no platform team, GitLaunch is deployment control everyone can drive. Unlike a stale spreadsheet and a brave engineer running scripts — or heavyweight tools built for platform teams — it shows exactly what's deployed in every environment and lets anyone promote or roll back in one click. So a bad deploy stops being a 3 a.m. crisis, and deploy day stops being a ritual to dread.

The six positioning components
01 — Competitive alternatives

What they'd do without you

  • A Notion page / spreadsheet of "what's deployed where," stale within a day
  • Slack archaeology: "hey, what's on staging right now?"
  • The one engineer who knows the deploy script — a single point of failure
  • Reading CI logs + git SHAs to reverse-engineer what's live
  • Heavyweight CD (Harness, Spinnaker, Octopus, Argo) — built for platform teams they don't have
02 — Unique attributes

What only you have

  • Situational awareness: one screen for what's where and what's ready
  • Democratized promote & rollback: anyone can move a build — no YAML, no kubectl
  • One-click rollback: undo a bad deploy in seconds, not a war room
  • Sits on top of your existing CI — nothing to rip out
03 — Value & proof

Why it matters

  • Deployment becomes calm, visible, anyone-can-do-it
  • Cuts lead time (code-complete → live) and MTTR (one-click rollback)
  • Kills the bus factor — delivery stops depending on one person
  • Proof: two DORA metrics move in week one; deploy-day incidents drop
04 — Who cares a lot

The target that feels it

  • Lean product teams — Series A–B startups, small scaleups, agencies
  • Ship frequently, deploy is bottlenecked on one or two people
  • No dedicated platform / DevOps / release team
  • Want QA, PMs, POs able to ship and roll back safely
Beachhead frequently-shipping startups on GitHub Actions or Jenkins
05 — Market category

The frame of reference

Release & deployment control for teams without a platform team.

Say-it-out-loud descriptor: "Continuous delivery your whole team can drive." This frame makes visibility, rollback, and democratization the obvious winning features — and puts the enterprise CD tools on a different shelf.

Category we refuse: CI/CD tool · pipeline automation
06 — Trend

Why now

Backlash against DIY platform complexity — "you shouldn't need a platform team to deploy safely."

Plus DORA metrics going mainstream — and you move two of the four directly.

What actually drives the buy
The 3 a.m. fear
"We can't say what's in production right now — and if it breaks, only one person can even roll it back."
Bus-factor fear
Deploys live in one person's head. If they're out, we're stuck — or worse, exposed.
Blindness fear
Nobody can confidently state what's actually live across environments right now.
Rollback fear
A bad deploy is bleeding revenue and reputation, and undo is slow and uncertain.

Market & next moves

Addressable market — sized bottom-up

Don't chase a fake TAM number. Size it as software teams that deploy regularly AND lack a platform team — the long tail of startups, scaleups, product shops, and agencies.

This is the underserved middle that enterprise CD vendors deliberately ignore because these buyers are "too small." That neglect is the whole opening.

Land at the beachhead — frequently-shipping startups already on GitHub Actions or Jenkins, where the pain is sharpest and the alternative is weakest — then expand outward.

Do these two things next

  1. Interview 5–10 of your happiest users. Listen for their exact words for the pain ("I never know what's on staging," "only Dave can deploy"). Real positioning is discovered in their language, not invented in a room.
  2. Kill the CI/CD framing everywhere. Site, deck, onboarding — reframe CI as a complement, and commit the whole team to this one shelf before writing a line of copy.
