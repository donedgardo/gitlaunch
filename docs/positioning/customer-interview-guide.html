Ground rules · read before every call
The Mom Test

Get truth, not compliments

The moment you pitch, you've contaminated the data.

  • Ask about the past, not the future. "Walk me through your last deploy" beats "Would you use a feature that…"
  • Don't pitch, teach, or defend. If they ask what you think: "I'll tell you after — I don't want to bias you."
  • Dig for the specific and recent. "The last time that happened" > "generally." Chase numbers, names, stories.
  • Capture exact words. Verbatim phrases are the deliverable — they become the copy.
  • Embrace the silence. Wait after they answer. The second thing they say is usually truer.
  • Record if allowed. Audio + notes, so you can mine the language later.
The conversation · ~30 minutes, ordered so you never pitch
01

Warm-up & context

≈4 min validates · Segment
  • What's your role, and how does your team ship software?
  • How often do you deploy, and to which environments?
  • Who's involved when something goes to production? Walk me through who touches it.
  • Do you have anyone whose job is deployment / release / platform / DevOps?
Listening for
Team size and shape, deploy frequency, and — critically — whether a platform team exists. The single most important segment signal.
02

Life before GitLaunch

≈6 min validates · Alternatives + Trigger
  • Before GitLaunch, how did you keep track of what was deployed where?
  • Take me back to the moment you started looking for something better. What happened that week?
  • What else did you try or seriously consider?Tools, a spreadsheet, a script, hiring someone, doing nothing?
  • Why didn't those work — or why not just keep doing what you were doing?
Listening for
The real competitive set. Spreadsheet + Slack + "the one person who knows" confirms the canvas; Octopus / Harness / Argo breaks it. The trigger event tells us what starts a buying journey.
03

Deploy-day stories & stakes

≈7 min validates · The Fear
  • Tell me about the last deploy that went wrong. What happened, and what did you do?
  • How did you figure out what was actually live at that moment?
  • When you needed to undo it — how did that go? Who could do it?
  • How do people feel on a deploy day?Is there a ritual, dread, a checklist, a designated person?
  • What's the worst-case version of a bad deploy for your business?
Listening for
The three fears — bus-factor ("only X can roll back"), blindness ("weren't sure what was live"), rollback ("undo was slow/scary"). Note which they reach for first and hottest — that's the fear to lead with.
04

Value in their words

≈6 min validates · Value + Attributes
  • What can you do now that you couldn't before GitLaunch?
  • What's the thing you'd miss most if we took it away tomorrow?
  • Has who's able to deploy or roll back changed? Who does it now?
  • Anything you expected it to help with that it doesn't?
Listening for
Whether they volunteer visibility and anyone-can-promote/rollback unprompted. "Who deploys now" is direct proof (or disproof) of the democratization claim.
05

Category & language

≈4 min validates · Category + Hook
  • If a peer asked "what's GitLaunch?", how would you describe it in a sentence?
  • What kind of tool is it, in your head? What "bucket" does it live in?
  • If GitLaunch disappeared tomorrow, what's the first thing you'd do?
Listening for
Do they say "CI/CD tool" (the shelf we're leaving) or something closer to "where we see and control what's deployed"? Their one-sentence description is a candidate headline.
06

Wrap

≈3 min validates · Reach
  • Who else on your team should I talk to?Especially a non-engineer who deploys.
  • Anything I should have asked but didn't?

Synthesizing across interviews
After ~8 conversations

Look for a pattern of language and a pattern of pain — not a vote count.

1 · Segment
Do the people who love us cluster on "no platform team, ships often"? If the enthusiasts skew elsewhere, follow the enthusiasts — that's the discovery.
2 · The words
Collect every verbatim phrase for pain and value. The most-repeated phrasing becomes the hook. If customers never say "deployment control," don't either.
3 · The dominant fear
Whichever fear shows up first and hottest is the one to build the sales narrative around.
4 · The alternative
If nobody names heavyweight CD tools, the fight is against spreadsheets + heroes — never position against Harness / Spinnaker in copy.

Output: update the Positioning Canvas with what you learned — the segment definition, the hook copy, and the lead fear — and note anything that broke. A canvas that survives contact with 8 real customers is worth building a homepage on. One that doesn't just saved you a rewrite.
