Who to interview · 8–12 across three buckets
A

Happiest users

5–6 interviews

The ones who'd be most upset if GitLaunch vanished. Highest signal for value and language.

B

Recent adopters

2–3 interviews

Signed up in the last 60 days. Fresh memory of the trigger and the alternatives they weighed.

C

Evaluated / churned

2–3 interviews

Tells us the segment boundary and where the value didn't land.

All must deploy software regularly. Where possible, talk to an engineer and a QA / PM / PO at the same account — the democratization claim lives or dies on non-engineers.

Ground rules · read before every call
The Mom Test

Get truth, not compliments

The moment you pitch, you've contaminated the data.

  • Ask about the past, not the future. "Walk me through your last deploy" beats "Would you use a feature that…"
  • Don't pitch, teach, or defend. If they ask what you think: "I'll tell you after — I don't want to bias you."
  • Dig for the specific and recent. "The last time that happened" > "generally." Chase numbers, names, stories.
  • Anchor to one team and one deploy — immediately. Take the opening question, then narrow before they can generalize: "let's take just your most recent team — walk me through the last thing that went to production." Interview 001 came back a career survey because this step was skipped. One org, one deploy, one week.
  • Capture exact words. Verbatim phrases are the deliverable — they become the copy.
  • Embrace the silence. Wait after they answer. The second thing they say is usually truer.
  • Record if allowed. Audio + notes, so you can mine the language later.
The conversation · ~35 minutes, ordered so you never pitch
01

Warm-up & context

≈5 min validates · Segment
  • What's your role, and how does your team ship software?
  • How often do you deploy, and to which environments?
  • Do you ship when something's ready, or do you batch into releases?Screener. Release branches and a stabilization window, or merge-and-it's-out?
  • Who's involved when something goes to production? Walk me through who touches it.
  • Do you have anyone whose job is deployment / release / platform / DevOps?
Listening for
Team size and shape, deploy frequency, and — critically — whether a platform team exists. The single most important segment signal. Also log batch vs. continuous: the beachhead assumes continuous, and Review 001 flagged that gitflow shops pull the other way. Track it; don't broaden the segment to chase it.
02

Life before GitLaunch

≈6 min validates · Alternatives + Trigger
  • Before GitLaunch, how did you keep track of what was deployed where?
  • Walk me to the exact place you'd look to find out what's running on staging right now.Bet 05. Don't accept "we'd check" — make them name the surface. A branch, a CI run, a person, or a page?
  • Take me back to the moment you started looking for something better. What happened that week?
  • When you want to ship something risky to a few users first — who sets that up, and where does it live today?Bet 07. A flag, a config value, an env var, a separate deploy — or you just don't do it?
  • What else did you try or seriously consider?Tools, a spreadsheet, a script, hiring someone, doing nothing?
  • Why didn't those work — or why not just keep doing what you were doing?
Listening for
The real competitive set. Spreadsheet + Slack + "the one person who knows" confirms the canvas; Octopus / Harness / Argo breaks it. The trigger event tells us what starts a buying journey. Two alternatives to listen for specifically: the branch graph as the believed record of what's live (bet 05), and "we hardcode it and redeploy" as the real substitute for flags (bet 07) — note that a named flag vendor is not the alternative we're testing for.
03

Deploy-day stories & stakes

≈5 min validates · The Fear
  • Tell me about the last deploy that went wrong. What happened, and what did you do?
  • How did you figure out what was actually live at that moment?
  • How do people feel on a deploy day?Is there a ritual, dread, a checklist, a designated person?
  • What's the worst-case version of a bad deploy for your business?
Listening for
The three fears — bus-factor ("only X can roll back"), blindness ("weren't sure what was live"), rollback ("undo was slow/scary"). Note which they reach for first and hottest — that's the fear to lead with. Hold onto the incident they name here; section 04 walks its clock.
04

The restore clock & the undo

≈7 min validates · Bets 06 · 08 · 09 · 10
  • Staying with that same incident — walk me through the clock. When did you first know something was wrong? When did you know why? When was it actually better again?Bet 09. Three timestamps, not a metric. If they don't remember exactly, ask for the shape: minutes, an hour, half a day?
  • Between "something's wrong" and "we know what to undo" — how much of that outage was which?Bet 10, and the one that matters most. Was the time spent finding the cause, or building and shipping the fix?
  • When you needed to undo it — how did that go? Who could do it?
  • Did you roll the deploy back, or fix forward?Bet 06. Then the question that actually matters: was that a choice, or the only option available to you?
  • Was there a way to switch just that one thing off without undoing the whole release? Which would you have preferred?Bet 08. If they'd prefer the narrow undo, ask what stops them. If flag-off sounds riskier, ask why.
  • What did that gap cost you?Bet 09. Money, a customer, a weekend, a call from someone senior.
Listening for
The clock, not the mechanism. Roll back, fix forward and flag-off are three routes to the same destination — the split tells us which route to name first in copy, nothing more. What decides the position is where the time went: if diagnose outweighs act (bet 10), the deploy×flag join is the product. Separate preference from constraint on fix-forward. And treat cost per minute of degradation as the real "who cares most" signal — it sorts prospects better than the branching model does.
05

Value in their words

≈5 min validates · Value + Attributes
  • What can you do now that you couldn't before GitLaunch?
  • What's the thing you'd miss most if we took it away tomorrow?
  • Has who's able to deploy or roll back changed? Who does it now?
  • Anything you expected it to help with that it doesn't?
Listening for
Whether they volunteer visibility and anyone-can-promote/rollback unprompted. "Who deploys now" is direct proof (or disproof) of the democratization claim.
06

Category & language

≈4 min validates · Category + Hook
  • If a peer asked "what's GitLaunch?", how would you describe it in a sentence?
  • What kind of tool is it, in your head? What "bucket" does it live in?
  • If GitLaunch disappeared tomorrow, what's the first thing you'd do?
Listening for
Do they say "CI/CD tool" (the shelf we're leaving) or something closer to "where we see and control what's deployed"? Their one-sentence description is a candidate headline.
07

Wrap

≈3 min validates · Reach
  • Who else on your team should I talk to?Especially a non-engineer who deploys.
  • Anything I should have asked but didn't?

Running long? Compress 02 — the history summarizes better afterwards than anything else here. Protect 04: it settles four of the six open bets, and a restore clock cannot be reconstructed from notes once the call ends.

Synthesizing across interviews
After ~8 conversations

Look for a pattern of language and a pattern of pain — not a vote count.

1 · Segment
Do the people who love us cluster on "no platform team, ships often"? If the enthusiasts skew elsewhere, follow the enthusiasts — that's the discovery.
2 · The words
Collect every verbatim phrase for pain and value. The most-repeated phrasing becomes the hook. If customers never say "deployment control," don't either.
3 · The dominant fear
Whichever fear shows up first and hottest is the one to build the sales narrative around.
4 · The alternative
If nobody names heavyweight CD tools, the fight is against spreadsheets + heroes — never position against Harness / Spinnaker in copy.
5 · The clock, not the mechanism
Do not tally rollback vs. fix-forward as a vote — it's a split in method, not in buyers, and both routes serve the same restore promise. Tally where the time went instead. If diagnose outweighs act across the set, the deploy×flag join is the product and the mechanism split is only a copy decision. If it doesn't, the rollback button is the product and bet 06 turns dangerous again. This is the one result that can reorder the canvas.

Output: update the Positioning Canvas with what you learned — the segment definition, the hook copy, and the lead fear — and note anything that broke. A canvas that survives contact with 8 real customers is worth building a homepage on. One that doesn't just saved you a rewrite.
